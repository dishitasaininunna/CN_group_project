6. HIGH vs NORMAL Traffic Performance Comparison

Tool: iperf3 over UDP
Topology: Mininet diamond topology
Duration: 20 seconds
Offered rate: 8 Mbps per traffic class
HIGH traffic: UDP destination port 5003, Queue 1
NORMAL traffic: UDP destination port 5004, Queue 2

Receiver-side results:
- HIGH throughput: 7.04 Mbps
- NORMAL throughput: 3.80 Mbps
- HIGH jitter: 1.454 ms
- NORMAL jitter: 3.808 ms
- HIGH packet loss: 0%
- NORMAL packet loss: 0%

Queue configuration:
- HIGH minimum rate: 7 Mbps
- NORMAL minimum rate: 1 Mbps
- Shared QoS maximum rate: 10 Mbps

OVS queue statistics on s1-eth2:
- Queue 1: 13,122 packets, 19,505,137 bytes
- Queue 2: 6,633 packets, 9,835,163 bytes

Observation:
HIGH traffic achieved approximately 1.85 times the receiver throughput of NORMAL traffic and had lower jitter in this experiment. The queue counters confirm that traffic was classified into separate queues. Both traffic classes experienced 0% packet loss in this run.

Note: These are measured results from one experimental run and may vary across runs.


Experiment 7: Baseline vs Proposed Performance Comparison

Test conditions:
- Mininet diamond topology

- Offered rate: 8 Mbps per flow

- Measurements taken from iperf3 receiver summaries
- One run per configuration

PROPOSED:
Controller: controller.py

HIGH (UDP port 5003):
Throughput: 6.86 Mbps

NORMAL (UDP port 5004):
Throughput: 2.66 Mbps
Jitter: 4.973 ms

COMPARISON:
- Baseline throughput was nearly equal for both traffic classes.
- Proposed HIGH traffic achieved 6.86 Mbps compared with 5.84 Mbps
  in the baseline run.
- Proposed NORMAL traffic received 2.66 Mbps, reflecting the
  prioritization of HIGH traffic.
- Both proposed flows recorded 0% packet loss in this run.
- NORMAL traffic jitter increased from 0.366 ms to 4.973 ms.

OpenFlow verification:
- UDP destination port 5003 used set_queue:1.
- UDP destination port 5004 used set_queue:2.
- Non-zero packet counters were observed for both queue rules.

The comparison represents one run per configuration. Results may vary
between runs; repeated trials would be needed for statistical conclusions.
